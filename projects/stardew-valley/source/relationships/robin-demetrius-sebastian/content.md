# Robin, Demetrius, and Sebastian

Robin, Demetrius, and Sebastian share one mountain house, but the marriage and the parenthood inside it never settled into one family shape. Robin is Sebastian's mother. Demetrius is Robin's wife and Sebastian's stepmother, a role Sebastian has never accepted. Sebastian lives below the household in the basement, close enough to hear every argument and far enough to leave without being stopped.

Robin wants a home where everyone can come upstairs without bracing for impact. She worries about Sebastian's isolation, still wants her approval, and keeps trying to offer food, questions, and practical help when listening would be harder. Demetrius wants to be treated as a legitimate adult in her own household and wants Sebastian to respect the work of the family around her. She also wants to protect Maru and her research from being made responsible for every old hurt. Sebastian wants her work and privacy to count as real life, not a phase to be managed. She wants Robin's care without being monitored and has no reason to grant Demetrius authority she has not earned.

The town sees Robin's busy shop, Demetrius's laboratory door, and the basement hatch. Neighbors hear a warm welcome from Robin, a precise correction from Demetrius, and Sebastian leaving through the side door. Maru often becomes the bridge between rooms, though nobody asks whether that is fair. Public politeness hides the fact that Sebastian does not call Demetrius a parent and that Robin's attempts to keep peace can feel like another demand.

There is no settled reconciliation. Sebastian might accept Demetrius, remain distant, or leave the house before either woman is ready. Demetrius might learn to stop steering, or mistake restraint for surrender. Robin might choose a side, refuse to choose, or finally say what the silence has cost her.

In a scene, this household turns any practical task into a pressure point: a broken machine, a research specimen, a workshop request, or a late-night noise can bring all three together while the old hierarchy remains unsettled.
