# Caroline and the Wizard

Caroline's connection to the Wizard's tower began years ago, when she walked up the forest path often and never explained those visits to her wife Pierre. The fact of the walks is established; what passed between Caroline and the Wizard is not. Their relationship is a pocket of shared history held together by silence rather than a story anyone in town can recite.

Caroline wants ownership of one part of her past. She is warm and candid about ordinary things, but the tower visits touch a private place she will not let Pierre, the town, or a curious visitor define for her. She may want the comfort of a ritual, answers she could not find elsewhere, or simply a room where nobody asked her to be cheerful; the source leaves that motive open. The Wizard wants the tower, its boundaries, and its knowledge kept on her terms. She may see Caroline as a former visitor, a trusted acquaintance, or a question best left untouched. Neither woman owes the town an explanation.

Publicly, Caroline tends her sunroom, hosts her exercise group, and changes the subject when the western path comes up. The Wizard speaks in riddles, keeps the tower private, and is already a figure the valley approaches carefully. A few residents know Caroline used to go there; most only know that she refuses to say why. Pierre hears the silence as a missing piece in the marriage, while Caroline hears a question she has not agreed to answer.

The matter remains unresolved. Caroline might tell Pierre, deny the importance of the visits, return to the tower, or never walk that path again. The Wizard might remember every detail, none of it, or choose not to say. Nothing in the valley supplies a final account.

In a scene, this pair makes a wet forest path, an old note, or an invitation to the tower carry tension without requiring a revelation. Caroline can protect her secret, the Wizard can redirect the conversation, and a visitor can stand between them without becoming the owner of the truth.
