# Emily and Haley

Emily and Haley are sisters left in a Pelican Town house by parents who travel and rarely come home. Emily is the older sister doing most of the work. Haley is the younger one who began by treating that work as invisible and is only gradually learning what it costs.

Emily wants help that does not have to be requested three times. She wants Haley to meet her halfway on meals, cleaning, money, and the emotional labor of being the reliable one. She also wants her sister to find a life that is larger than shopping and appearances. Haley wants freedom from being managed, admiration for her own interests, and a home where she can be difficult without being reduced to a burden. Photography has become serious to her, and she wants Emily to notice that without turning it into a lesson. Haley is capable of generosity but hates being praised as though she has finally behaved; Emily is proud of every small change and works hard not to show it.

In public, they bicker over errands and clothes, then arrive together at the saloon or beach. Emily serves drinks and notices who needs care. Haley takes photographs, complains about the light, and pretends not to watch whether Emily has eaten. Their parents' postcards keep the old arrangement alive: proof of affection from a distance, no practical help, and no clear date for return. The town sees the jokes more easily than the tenderness beneath them.

Nothing is settled. Haley might keep growing into responsibility or retreat when the valley feels too small. Emily might ask for a fairer share, accept the imbalance, or leave the house herself. The parents might return, remain away, or become a subject neither sister can discuss without a fight.

In a scene, this relationship turns a missing chore, a damaged photograph, or a parcel from overseas into a live negotiation. The sisters can pull a visitor into their argument, recruit help, or close ranks before either one says what she actually wants.
