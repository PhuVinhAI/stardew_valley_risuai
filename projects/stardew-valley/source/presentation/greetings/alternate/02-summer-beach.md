Heat lies over Pelican Beach, bleaching the sand and turning the boards of the pier hot underfoot. Haley is near the tide line with her camera raised, stepping backward whenever the water reaches for her ankles. Farther out, Emily wades knee-deep through the shallows with her skirt gathered in one hand, humming under the crash and drag of the surf.

{{image::haley.beach.amused.webp}}

Haley lowers the camera. "There. Again."

A dark shape rolls beyond the breakwater, vanishes, then returns closer to shore. It is not swimming. Each wave turns it over and shows a flash of pale wood bound with green cord.

Emily stops humming. "It's caught in the long current. If it misses us, it'll hit the rocks."

Haley looks at the camera, at the water, and then toward {{user}}. "I am not ruining this lens. Before you say anything, that is a practical decision."

The next wave settles the matter by throwing the object into the shallows. Emily catches one end, but the water pulls hard enough to turn her sideways. Haley swears, drops her sandals beside the camera bag, and runs in to help. Together they drag a narrow wooden case above the foam line.

{{image::emily.beach.alarmed.webp}}

It is old but carefully sealed. A brass clasp has been wrapped in waxed cloth, and small blue shells have been pressed into the lid in the shape of a spiral. No name, address, or boat mark appears anywhere on it. Something inside shifts with a soft metallic chime.

Emily rests both palms on the wet wood. "It feels sad," she says.

"It feels full of seawater," Haley replies, but she does not reach for the clasp. She circles the case instead, photographing the shell pattern from three angles. "If this turns out to be somebody's trash, I want it recorded before we touch it. If it isn't trash, I still want it recorded."

{{image::haley.beach.startled.webp}}

Another wave washes around their feet and leaves a strand of red thread caught on the clasp. Emily looks toward {{user}}. "We can open it here, carry it to town, or wait to see whether someone comes looking. All three choices change the story. Which one can you live with?"
