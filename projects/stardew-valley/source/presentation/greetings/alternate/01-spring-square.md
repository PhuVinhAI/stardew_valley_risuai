Spring rain has left the town square shining. Water ticks from the fountain's stone rim, the flower beds smell of dark soil, and the notice board has swollen just enough that every pin sits crooked. Evelyn kneels among the tulips with a basket beside her. Caroline crosses from the store carrying a tray of seedlings under one arm and a folded cloth over her hair.

{{image::evelyn.default.fond.webp}}

"Don't pull that one," Evelyn says without looking up.

Caroline stops with her fingers an inch from a green shoot. "I wasn't going to."

"You were thinking about it very loudly."

Their argument is gentle until a gust lifts every loose page on the notice board at once. Grocery offers, requests for help, a hand-drawn festival reminder, and one of Pierre's advertisements slap against the wood. A smaller square of violet paper tears free and skates across the wet cobbles toward {{user}}.

Caroline catches her seedling tray against her hip. "That wasn't there when I opened the shop."

{{image::caroline.default.downcast.webp}}

The note carries no signature. Its ink has run at the edges, but the middle remains legible: *Midnight. Bring something purple. Come alone.* Beneath the words is a rough drawing of three standing stones and a crescent moon.

Evelyn rises slowly, brushing soil from her knees. The warmth leaves neither her face nor her voice, but she studies the symbol longer than Caroline does. "There used to be marks like that near the old path," she says. "Before the bushes grew over it."

"Or Abigail found violet paper and decided the town needed excitement," Caroline answers. She says it lightly, then glances toward the upstairs windows of the store.

Across the square, Pierre opens the front door and calls that rain is getting into the flour display. Caroline does not move. Evelyn holds out a clean gardening cloth toward {{user}}, offering it for the wet note.

"You found it," she says. "That doesn't make it your responsibility. But before the whole square starts guessing, did you see where it came from?"
