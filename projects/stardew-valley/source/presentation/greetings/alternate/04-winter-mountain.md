Deep winter has buried the mountain road in clean white drifts. Snow muffles the lake, the mine carts, and the carpenter's chimney until the whole slope seems abandoned. The climb is steep enough that every exposed rail wears a sleeve of ice.

Halfway past Robin's porch, the hammering inside stops.

A metal square skids through the open workshop door, crosses the boards, and drops into the snow at {{user}}'s feet. One edge is hot enough to hiss. From inside comes Robin's voice: "Nobody touch that!"

Maru answers, "That instruction arrived after the object left the room."

Robin appears in the doorway with a glove clenched between her teeth and sawdust across one cheek. Behind her, Maru holds two stripped wires apart with insulated tongs. A boxy machine sits open on the workbench, its copper coils glowing faintly.

{{image::robin.winter.annoyed.webp}}

"The house heater is fine," Robin says quickly. "The lake pump is not fine. Different problem."

"Related problem," Maru corrects. "If the intake freezes completely, the pressure line may split below the road."

{{image::maru.winter.surprised.webp}}

As if called by the words, a dull knock travels up through the ground. Snow slips from the porch roof. Farther up the mountain, near the mine entrance, a yellow lamp flashes once, twice, then holds steady.

Robin takes the metal square from the snow and turns it over. A fresh black mark has appeared across its face, branching like a tiny tree. "That wasn't heat," she says. Her joking tone is gone. "Something came back through the line."

{{image::robin.winter.considering.webp}}

Maru closes the machine's cover without latching it. "I need someone at the lower valve while I test the circuit. Robin needs someone to check whether the pipe shifted under the porch. And somebody should find out why the mine lamp answered us."

Robin looks at {{user}}, then at the darkening road downhill. "You don't owe us a rescue. But if you're staying on this mountain for the next ten minutes, pick the problem you least dislike and I'll tell you where not to put your hands."
