Night rain runs in silver lines down the Stardrop Saloon windows. Gus dries a glass behind the bar, Emily counts coins at a corner table, and Shane sits at the far end with one hand around a drink she has not touched. The jukebox hums between songs while water drips from {{user}}'s coat onto the worn floorboards.

{{image::gus.default.attentive.webp}}

"Leave it by the stove if you want," Gus says, nodding toward the coat. "The hook leaks less than the roof."

Before {{user}} can answer, the back door slams hard enough to rattle every bottle on the shelf.

Emily looks up. "That wasn't the wind."

Shane turns on her stool and studies the room rather than the door. An overturned chair lies near the pool table. One cue is missing. Muddy footprints begin beside the table, cross the floor, and end at the locked kitchen entrance. There are no prints from the front door to match them.

{{image::shane.default.glance-aside.webp}}

"Someone left in a hurry," Shane says.

Gus sets the glass down. "Nobody left through my kitchen."

The jukebox clicks and starts a song none of them selected. It plays for four seconds, slows into a low warped note, and stops. From the other side of the locked door comes the sound of something being placed carefully on the floor.

Emily rises, coins forgotten. Her expression is calm, but she moves around the table rather than straight toward the noise. "There's a person back there," she says. "Or something that wants us to think like one."

{{image::emily.default.alarmed.webp}}

Shane slides off the stool. "Great. Those are my two favorite options."

Gus reaches under the bar and brings out a heavy wooden rolling pin. She looks to {{user}}, measuring neither courage nor loyalty, only whether another pair of hands is present. "You can stay by the front door, come with us, or walk out before we unlock anything. No shame in any of them. But decide now."

The kitchen key hangs from her finger. Beneath the door, a thin line of rainwater begins to spread across the floor from the dry room beyond.
