The saloon is loud enough to hide a private argument and small enough that everyone knows one is happening. Emily moves between tables with a tray on one hand. Pam occupies the stool she considers hers. Shane sits at the counter's far end. Clint has spent so long chalking a pool cue that the chalk has become an alibi.

Gus works behind the bar with the careful speed of a woman preventing three separate disasters.

"If either of you breaks that cue, you are paying for it."

Pam taps the bar with two fingers.

"Put it on my tab."

Emily sets down a glass.

"Your tab is not a form of municipal funding."

"It is if the mayor drinks here."

"The mayor pays," Gus says.

Shane lifts her eyes.

"Eventually."

The front door opens as {{user}} reaches it, pushed from the other side by a gust of warm evening air. At the same moment the pool table gives a sharp wooden crack. Clint has not taken a shot. The balls roll apart by themselves, slow at the cushions, and settle into a rough circle around the center spot.

Conversation fades table by table. Emily is the first to move closer.

"Nobody touch them yet."

Pam leans over the rail anyway.

"They're pool balls, not eggs."

"That's what you said about the thing in Marnie's incubator," Shane replies.

Clint puts the cue down very carefully.

"The table isn't level. It could be vibration from the cellar. Or the road. Or the heating pipes."

Gus looks beneath the table.

"I don't have a cellar."

One ball turns in place. Its number disappears under it, then returns facing {{user}}. A second ball does the same. Then a third.

Emily counts them softly.

"Three. Eleven. Eight."

Pam grins, suddenly delighted.

"A bet, then."

"Not everything strange is asking for money," Gus says.

"Most things in this room are."

The argument resumes around the impossible circle, each woman pulling it toward her own explanation — Gus wants the room calm, Emily wants everyone paying attention, Clint wants tools and measurements, Pam wants to test the pattern.
