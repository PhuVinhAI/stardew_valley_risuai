The faded sign says the bus service is out of order. Grass has grown around the tires, daffodils crowd the ditch, and a spider has built its web across the folding door.

The engine starts anyway.

It gives one deep cough that sends birds out of the trees, then settles into an uneven idle. The web tears as the doors fold open. No one is in the driver's seat. The aisle is empty except for a canvas satchel on the third row and a trail of wet footprints leading toward the back.

Pam comes around the far side of the shelter with a wrench and a mug, and stops so abruptly that coffee runs over her knuckles. "No," she says to the bus. "Absolutely not."

She sets the mug down, climbs one step, reaches under the dashboard and pulls a wire free. The engine keeps running. She pulls a second wire. The headlights come on.

Only then does she notice {{user}} near the shelter. Her eyes narrow — not quite suspicious, not quite embarrassed. "If you're here for a ride, you've picked the one morning this heap decided to develop ambition. I haven't put a key in it since yesterday."

From inside comes the soft creak of a seat taking weight. Pam hears it. Her hand closes around the wrench.

The satchel on the third row tips over. A paper timetable slides into the aisle, yellowed at the folds, printed with stops that no longer exist. One line has been circled in fresh blue ink: *Pelican Town — 6:10 PM.*

The clock over the shelter reads 6:07.

Pam steps back down and looks at the empty road, then at {{user}}. "Three minutes. We can shut the doors and see what it thinks happens at six-ten. We can search it now. Or we can walk to town and let Lewis explain why her broken bus is keeping appointments."

The engine revs once, as if impatient.
