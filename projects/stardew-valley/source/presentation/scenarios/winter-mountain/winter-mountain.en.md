Deep winter has buried the mountain road in clean white drifts. Snow muffles the lake, the mine carts, the carpenter's chimney, until the whole slope seems abandoned. Every exposed rail wears a sleeve of ice.

Halfway past the porch, the hammering inside stops.

A metal square skids out through the open workshop door, crosses the boards, and drops into the snow at {{user}}'s feet. One edge is hot enough to hiss.

{{image::robin.winter.annoyed.webp}}

Robin appears in the doorway with a glove clenched between her teeth and sawdust across one cheek.

"Nobody touch that!" She takes the glove out. "The house heater is fine. The lake pump is not fine. Different problem."

{{image::maru.winter.surprised.webp}}

Behind her, Maru holds two stripped wires apart with insulated tongs, a boxy machine open on the bench with its copper coils glowing faint.

"Related problem. If the intake freezes completely, the pressure line may split below the road."

As if called by the words, a dull knock travels up through the ground. Snow slips off the porch roof. Farther up the mountain, near the mine entrance, a yellow lamp flashes once, twice, then holds steady.

{{image::robin.winter.considering.webp}}

Robin picks the metal square out of the snow and turns it over. A fresh black mark has appeared across its face, branching like a small tree.

"That wasn't heat." The joking tone is gone. "Something came back through the line."

Maru closes the machine's cover without latching it.

"I need someone at the lower valve while I test the circuit. Robin needs someone to check whether the pipe shifted under the porch. And somebody should find out why the mine lamp answered us."

Robin looks at {{user}}, then down the darkening road.

"You don't owe us a rescue. But if you're staying on this mountain another ten minutes — pick the problem you least dislike and I'll tell you where not to put your hands."
