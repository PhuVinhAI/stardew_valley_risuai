Deep winter, and the light is going out of the mountain fast. The lake below the house has been frozen solid for three weeks, a flat grey plate under the snow, and the cold coming off it has a shape to it — it finds the gap between a collar and a scarf and settles there. The air is so still that the pines hold their loads without shifting. Every breath tastes of iron and woodsmoke, and the only warm thing anywhere on the slope is the low gold spill of the carpenter's windows across the drifts.

Robin has been lying on her side in the snow beside the porch for forty minutes with one arm down a hole she dug herself, and she has lost the feeling in three fingers, and she is nearly finished with today.

The intake line from the lake froze sometime last night. She knows what she is doing — she built this house, she laid this pipe, she remembers exactly how deep it sits and what it is bedded in — and none of that helps when the coupling is under a foot of packed snow and the wrench will not seat on a nut she cannot see. She got the last quarter turn out of it by feel. Then the wrench slipped, and her knuckles went into the ice, and she said something long and heartfelt about the pipe, the coupling, the lake, and the woman she was when she decided to run the line this way in the first place.

She hauls herself up onto one elbow, snow packed down her whole front, and sees a stranger standing at the bottom of her track in the last of the daylight.

"Well, that's — hold on. Hold on, don't stand there in it. Sorry. You're getting me at the absolute worst part of a very stupid afternoon, and I look like I lost a fight with a snowdrift, which I did."

Maru is up on the porch step with a work lamp and a thermos, and she has been having a considerably better time of it than her mother. She got the good job: hold the light, hand down tools, and offer engineering commentary from a warm doorway. She swings the lamp round and the beam finds {{user}}.

{{image::maru.winter.cheeky.webp}}

"Hi. Ignore about ninety percent of what she just said, the pipe deserved most of it."

She comes down two steps for a better look, boots squeaking on the dry snow, entirely unable to keep the delight off her face — at the visitor, and at the state her mother is in, roughly equally.

"Nobody walks up here in winter. Nobody. We get the bus road, we get the mailbox, and that's the social calendar until spring, so you have no idea how interesting you are right now."

"She's not exaggerating," Robin says.

She sits up properly and knocks the snow off her forearms.

"I've talked to a frozen pipe more than I've talked to a new face since the first snowfall. That's not a complaint about the pipe. The pipe listens."

The cold takes its opening while they are all standing still. It comes up through boot soles, it works into cuffs, and it turns the wet patch on Robin's shirt front stiff enough to crackle when she moves. Down at the lake edge the ice makes a single deep note, settling, the way it does every evening about now.

Maru feels her mother's shiver from three feet away and switches, without any fuss, out of teasing and into the practical register she uses at the clinic.

"Right, that's enough of this. Mom, you've been down there since before four and you can't feel your hands, so the coupling can wait ten minutes and so can the pipe."

"I can feel two of them," Robin says.

She gets her boots under her, which takes a moment, and looks {{user}} over with the frank appraising once-over she gives a stranger, a joist, or a load-bearing wall.

{{image::robin.winter.greeting.webp}}

"Fine. Ten minutes. Come inside where it's warm and tell me who you are, because if I stand out here explaining myself I'll lose the other two fingers."

She takes the lamp off Maru, sets it on the porch rail so it throws light down over the steps for whoever is coming up them, and holds the door with her shoulder against the dark.

"And if you're the kind of person who doesn't mind holding a wrench for somebody afterward — the stove's lit, there's coffee that's been on it too long, and I'd take the help and not pretend otherwise."
