The square is still shining. Water ticks off the fountain's stone rim, the flower beds smell of dark soil, and the notice board has swollen just enough that every pin sits crooked.

Evelyn kneels among the tulips with a basket beside her, not looking up.

"Don't pull that one."

Caroline stops with her fingers an inch from a green shoot, a tray of seedlings under her other arm.

"I wasn't going to."

"You were thinking about it very loudly."

A gust lifts every loose page on the notice board at once. Grocery offers, requests for help, a hand-drawn festival reminder, one of Pierre's advertisements — all of it slaps against the wood. A smaller square of violet paper tears free and skates across the wet cobbles to {{user}}.

Caroline catches her tray against her hip.

"That wasn't there when I opened the shop."

No signature. The ink has run at the edges, but the middle is legible: *Midnight. Bring something purple. Come alone.* Below the words, a rough drawing of three standing stones and a crescent moon.

Evelyn rises slowly, brushing soil from her knees. The warmth stays in her face and her voice, but she studies the symbol longer than Caroline does.

"There used to be marks like that near the old path. Before the bushes grew over it."

"Or Abigail found violet paper and decided the town needed excitement," Caroline says. Lightly — then she glances up at the store's second-floor windows.

Across the square Pierre opens the front door and calls that rain is getting into the flour display. Caroline does not move.

Evelyn holds out a clean gardening cloth toward {{user}}, offering it for the wet note.

"You found it. That doesn't make it your responsibility."

"But before the whole square starts guessing — did you see where it came from?"
