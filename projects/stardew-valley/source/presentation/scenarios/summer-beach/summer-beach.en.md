Heat lies flat over the beach. The sand is bleached, the pier boards are hot underfoot, and the surf keeps up its long crash and drag.

{{image::haley.beach.amused.webp}}

Haley is near the water with her camera raised, stepping back whenever a wave reaches for her ankles. She lowers the lens.

"There. Again."

A dark shape rolls beyond the breakwater, vanishes, returns closer in. It is not swimming. Each wave turns it over and shows a flash of pale wood bound with green cord.

{{image::emily.beach.alarmed.webp}}

Emily stops humming, knee-deep with her skirt gathered in one hand.

"It's caught in the long current. If it misses us, it'll hit the rocks."

Haley looks at the camera, then the water, then {{user}}.

"I am not ruining this lens. Before you say anything — that is a practical decision."

The next wave settles it by throwing the thing into the shallows. Emily catches one end and the undertow turns her sideways. Haley swears, drops her sandals beside the camera bag, and runs in. Together they drag a narrow wooden case above the foam line.

It is old but carefully sealed: a brass clasp wrapped in waxed cloth, small blue shells pressed into the lid in a spiral. No name, no address, no boat mark. Something inside shifts with a soft metallic chime.

Emily rests both palms on the wet wood.

"It feels sad."

"It feels full of seawater," Haley says — but she doesn't reach for the clasp. She circles the case instead, photographing the shell pattern from three angles. "If this is somebody's trash, I want it recorded before we touch it. If it isn't trash, I still want it recorded."

Another wave washes around their feet and leaves a strand of red thread caught on the clasp.

Emily looks up at {{user}}.

"We can open it here, carry it to town, or wait and see whether someone comes looking. All three change the story. Which one can you live with?"
