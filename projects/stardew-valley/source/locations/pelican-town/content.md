# Pelican Town

## What it is

The only town in the valley: about a dozen buildings around a cobbled square, a river cutting through it, and a population small enough that everyone knows whose footsteps those are. Most of the valley's thirty-odd women live here or within sight of it. Nobody locks much.

## The place itself

The square holds a fountain, benches, flower beds that Evelyn and Caroline keep and Lewis takes credit for, and the notice board where the mayor and Pierre pin whatever the town is supposed to know. Pierre's store faces it; the saloon is a short walk east; the clinic and the archaeological office sit north of the square, the blacksmith and the mayor's manor east of that.

The river runs down the middle, crossed by bridges, and continues south to the sea. West of the river are the houses on Willow Lane and River Road. East is the corporate warehouse store, or the cinema, depending on how the town's fight over the community center went. South, past the graveyard, steps and a bridge drop to the beach; north, three separate paths climb toward the mountain; west, a gate opens onto the road to the bus stop.

The graveyard is small, walled, and older than anyone remembers. One headstone is worn past reading. Another says only *Our Beloved Mona*. A third is cut in dwarvish, and anyone who can read it finds a verse: stand between three pillars with a gift as precious as the sky, a rainbow forged from land rather than sea, and the galaxies will answer. Nobody in town can read it. Under the graveyard there is a hatch down into the sewers, locked with a rusted key. Behind the square there is a public green and a bush above it that a rumour insists holds something at noon on the last day of a season.

In summer someone runs the ice cream stand in town whenever they can be bothered, and it is closed the rest of the year. Some days a travelling bookseller sets up in town with expensive, strange volumes. The bins behind the buildings are a genuine food source for anyone who needs one, and everybody knows Linus uses them.

## Life here

Mornings are shop doors and deliveries; afternoons are people crossing the square on errands and stopping too long to talk; evenings drain toward the saloon. Rain empties the streets. Winter turns the whole place quiet, snow-muffled, and lit from inside windows.

The town square hosts the spring egg hunt, the autumn fair, and the lantern-lit night at the end of fall when the whole town goes out in costume; the winter feast happens here too, under snow.

## Roleplay notes

Use the town for encounters, gossip, and the friction of being seen. Anything that happens in the square is public within a day. Nothing here runs to a clock: shops keep rough hours, festivals happen if the story wants them, and characters can be anywhere for their own reasons.
