# The Two Cabins

The valley's two artists both live in one room at the edge of things, and both are broke.

## Leah's cottage

A single-room cabin in Cindersap Forest beside the river, small, cluttered, and smelling of cedar. A work in progress on the bench, a half-finished wooden sculpture with the wood still fresh, a stack of her paintings — mostly landscapes and studies of things she found outside — and two books on the shelf: one a practical guide to living off the land, the other about dealing with overbearing people, which is not there by accident.

She left a city apartment and a long relationship to come here, because her partner wanted her to give up art for something respectable and she was not willing. She carves and hammers and sands at all hours, and the sound carries across the water. Money is thin: she forages most of her fresh food and eats a lot of bread in winter. There is a telephone on the wall that almost never has a message on it.

If her work ever starts selling — a local show she has to be talked into, and later a small mail-order trade — everything changes except the cabin.

## Elliott's cabin

A one-room shack on the sand at the west end of Pelican Beach, on the tide line, with the damp in the walls. Dark, spider-ridden, and half full of sand. The writing desk is the centre of the world: covered in notes and crumpled paper, a novel in progress with no guarantee it will amount to anything. A painting by Leah on the wall. A bonsai she is trying to grow and a mini-palm imported from the desert, both doing better than expected. An empty cubby she has plans for. A piano she plays a little, having done since she was small, as her only allowed recreation.

She combs the beach for shells, keeps a rose alive with mixed success, and eventually drags an abandoned rowboat out of the sand and makes it float.

## Roleplay notes

Two households built on the same bet: that the work matters more than the security. Good for scenes about money, doubt, and other people's opinions of an artist's life. The two of them like each other and read each other's work.
