# Mayor's Manor

## What it is

The largest house in Pelican Town, on the east side of the square, where Lewis lives alone and conducts the whole business of the valley from her front room. Open through the day and most of the evening, because she does not distinguish between her house and her office.

## The place itself

Formal and slightly overdressed. A traditional tea setting she is serious about. An oven that could use cleaning. A fridge mostly full of milk and iced tea. On a stand, the Pelican Town ledger book, in which she tracks the valley's finances by hand, and beside it the marriage log, where the town's unions are recorded and — should anyone need it, for a fee that is deliberately punitive — dissolved. A lost-and-found box holding things people have handed in and never claimed. A prize machine she keeps for the fair.

Tucked out of sight there is a letter, unsigned but for an initial, asking her to come by tomorrow night, to use the back window so nobody notices, and to make time. She has kept it.

There is a basement, and she does not invite people down there.

## Who is there

Lewis, working every day of the week by her own account. Anyone with a complaint about a neighbour, a question about the budget, a festival to argue over, or a business tax to pay — she collects those personally. Marnie, occasionally, and never through the front door.

## Life here

Morning tea, a particular breakfast on a particular weekday, an evening round to collect what local producers have put out for shipping, and one night a week at the saloon to hear the news. She has been mayor for over twenty years, has never been opposed at an election, and treats that as proof rather than a warning. She corresponds with the regional governor and is desperate to impress her.

At some point somebody discovers a pair of purple shorts belonging to the mayor in a house she has no official reason to be in. She would like that never mentioned again.

## Roleplay notes

Use the manor for the town's official face and Lewis's private life pressing against it. She is genuinely devoted to this valley and genuinely a bit ridiculous; both at once. Do not run taxes, budgets, or a bureaucracy system.
