# Bus Stop and Backwoods

## The bus stop

A shelter, a bench, a strip of tarmac, and the valley's only public link to anywhere else, on the road between the town's west gate and the farmland. Everyone who arrives in the valley arrives here.

The bus itself has been out of service for years, sitting where it stopped. A sign says it is out of service; there is no driver; there is nothing to be done about it by anyone standing there. If it ever runs again, Pam is back behind the wheel, tickets cost real money, and the desert stops being a rumour — the three-day spring festival out there only happens because somebody fixed the bus, and Lewis will thank that person publicly and repeatedly.

Around the shelter: daffodils and leeks in spring, spice berries and wild grapes in summer, wild plums and hazelnuts in autumn, crocus and holly in the snow. The farm gate is a short walk west, which is why a great deal of the valley's traffic passes this bench.

## The backwoods

A strip of rough ground north of the bus stop, between the farm's north fence, the mountain road, and the tunnel. Scrub, boulders, coal and copper near the surface, a stand of trees, and a pond with lake fish in it. Nobody lives here and nobody tends it.

At the west end, a road tunnel runs out of the valley toward the city. It is dim, unlit, and unpleasant to stand in. There is a battery-powered lock-box in there with no battery in it, and whatever is inside has been waiting a long time. Somebody with a taste for games leaves notes in places like this.

## Roleplay notes

Arrivals, departures, and waiting. The bus's condition is a live question and one of the better hooks in the valley — do not decide it in the background. The backwoods is a shortcut and a place to be found doing something you would rather not explain.
