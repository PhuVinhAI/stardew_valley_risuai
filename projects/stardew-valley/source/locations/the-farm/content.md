# The Farm

## What it is

The old farmstead west of the bus stop and north of the forest: a few acres, a small cottage, and whatever the last generation left standing. It has been empty long enough for the fields to go to grass, brush and fallen wood, and the town has strong feelings about it either way.

## The place itself

A one-room cottage with a hearth, a bed, and a mailbox by the door. Outside: a shipping bin the mayor empties on her nightly round, a stone-lined well, and land in every direction — tillable ground, tree stands, boulders, a stream depending on which corner of the valley the plot lies in. The valley has several old holdings like this and they differ: one runs to river and gravel, one to forest and stumps, one to open hillside with ore in it, one down onto sand.

Behind the house, a cave in the hillside — cool, dark, and useful to whoever decides what to do with it. Below the kitchen, a cellar. Up the slope, the frame of a greenhouse with its glass gone, standing in ruins until somebody rebuilds it. Somewhere in the trees there is a small stone shrine the family left, addressed to a grandchild, with instructions and a date.

## What it gives

Whatever is put into it. Wild ground turns up coal, stone and stray seed; the untended parts spread weeds fast and swallow the paths again over winter. Buildings — coop, barn, shed, mill, extension — are Robin's work and Robin's fee.

## Life here

Solitary by nature and social by accident: Robin turns up with timber, Marnie about a stray animal, Willy about a fishing spot, Lewis about the bin, and Pierre about anything she can sell you. The bus stop is a short walk east, so the whole valley passes near the gate.

## Roleplay notes

Do not assume the user farms this land or owns it. It may be theirs, a neighbour's, abandoned, half-restored, or simply the place they walk past. Do not track crops, seasons, or money unless the story asks for them.
