# Stardrop Saloon

## What it is

The only bar in the valley and the room where the whole town eventually ends up. Gus owns it, cooks in it, and runs the counter from midday until midnight. Emily works the floor most evenings.

## The place itself

One large room: bar along one side with the kitchen behind it, tables and booths, a pool table nobody plays seriously, a jukebox, a cola machine that takes coins and gives nothing worth having, and two arcade cabinets in the corner — a punishing cowboy shooter and a minecart game — that Abigail and Sam treat as a standing competition. A chalked dish of the day changes daily and is genuinely good. There is beer, coffee, an espresso strong enough to be a dare, pizza, salad, spaghetti, omelettes, pancakes, hashbrowns, maki rolls, crab cakes, and bread.

Out the back there is a bin, and the day's unsold dish sometimes ends up in it still edible, which is not lost on anyone who needs it.

## Who is there

Gus behind the bar. Emily on the floor. Shane on a stool by the counter most nights, drinking steadily. Pam on the stool she considers hers, with a tab. Clint at the same table, alone, watching Emily and saying nothing. Marnie in the evening because animals are not enough company. Willy, Lewis one night a week for the news, Harvey rarely and awkwardly, Abigail and Sam and Sebastian at the arcade or a booth. George comes up when there is a game on and it makes her feel lively.

## Life here

Loud in the evening and dead in the afternoon. The town's news, feuds, and confessions all pass through this room, usually in the wrong order. Somebody is always slightly drunker than they meant to be. Gus notices and manages it without comment.

## Roleplay notes

The best place in the valley for an unplanned encounter, an overheard remark, or a conversation that goes further than intended. Hours are soft — if a scene wants the saloon open, it is open. Do not narrate a menu; narrate what the room is like tonight.
