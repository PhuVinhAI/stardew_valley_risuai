# The Mines

## What it is

An abandoned commercial mine cut into the mountainside above the lake, and the only genuinely dangerous place in the valley that anyone visits on purpose. It goes down a hundred and twenty levels and nobody has found a floor beneath that. Marlon's guild sits at the entrance because someone has to.

## The place itself

A timbered shaft, a lift cage that still works and remembers which floors you have reached, and level after level of worked-out rock. The character of it changes with depth: dirt and stone near the surface, then cold and ice through the middle, then heat and dark red rock deep down. Ore in the walls — copper, iron, gold, and further down things the blacksmith will only see a few times in her life. Geodes to be cracked, gems in seams, and old ladders that go down further than they should.

Things live down there. Slimes in every colour, bats, cave insects, animated stone, crab-like things that sit still until they do not, skeletons in the deep levels, ghosts in the cold ones. Nobody argues about whether the monsters are real; you can hear them.

At the bottom of the last level, someone eventually finds a key shaped like a skull. It fits a door in the desert.

## Who is there

Marlon at the entrance, selling weapons, boots and rings, keeping a bounty board, and running a paid retrieval service for the possessions of people who did not walk out under their own power. Gil in the chair behind her, asleep. Clint buys ore from anyone who brings it up, and has cleared monsters herself exactly as many times as she claims. Abigail practices with a sword and goes in further than her parents know. Somewhere much deeper, a small figure with a lamp trades with anyone who can understand what she says, and she is not human.

## Life here

Cold air out of the entrance, water dripping, the lift chain rattling. People go in for ore, for money, for nerve, or to prove something, and a few come out worse. Marlon sweeps the area in the small hours and has carried more than one unconscious stranger home. Linus has done the same, without being asked and without mentioning it afterwards.

## Roleplay notes

Real danger, real consequence: injury, exhaustion, getting lost, needing help. Do not run mine levels as a progression system or track loot. The valley's older strangeness — dwarves, an earlier civilisation, whatever the deep floors are — lives down here and should stay half-explained.
