# Valley Geography

## The shape of the place

Stardew Valley is a small inland region on the coast: a town, a river running down to the sea, farmland west of town, forest to the south, mountains to the north, and a beach at the bottom. Everything anyone needs is inside a long walk, and everything anyone wants is not. The whole valley is women; that is simply the population, not a remark anyone makes.

Pelican Town sits in the middle. Walk west out of the square and the road reaches the bus stop, and beyond that the farmland. Walk north and the road climbs to the mountain, the lake, and the mine. Walk south from the square and the ground drops to the beach and the pier. Walk south-west and you are in Cindersap Forest, which runs from the farm's south fence down to the ranch and the wizard's tower.

## The connections that matter in a scene

- **Town to bus stop** — west, through the gate by the ice cream stand; a few minutes on foot.
- **Town to mountain** — north, three separate ways up out of the north edge of town; a proper climb.
- **Town to beach** — south, down the steps past the graveyard and over the bridge.
- **Town to forest** — west and south along the river.
- **Forest to farm** — north across the farm's south fence line.
- **Bus stop to farm** — a short walk, and the reason the bus stop matters.
- **Backwoods** — the strip of scrub between the farm, the mountain road and the bus stop, with a road tunnel at its west end.
- **Mountain to railroad** — north again, up past the lake to the tracks, then higher still to the summit.
- **Forest to the secret woods** — through the trees at the far west edge, past fallen timber.
- **Beyond the valley** — the bus at the bus stop runs to Calico Desert when it runs at all; the tracks carry trains that do not stop; the road out goes toward Zuzu City; a boat behind the fish shop reaches an island in the south sea.

## Distance and time

Nothing in the valley is far, but everything is uphill or downhill. The mountain is a climb, the desert is hours, and the island is a sea trip. Walking between town and any farm gate is normal and unremarkable; walking to the desert is not possible. Weather changes the calculus more than distance does — rain closes the beach path in feeling if not in fact, snow makes the mountain miserable, and a storm keeps most of the town indoors.

## Roleplay notes

Use geography for texture, not for bookkeeping. Characters know these routes the way people know their own town: by landmark, by who they will pass, and by what the walk is like in each season. Do not narrate travel as a menu of destinations, and do not require the calendar or a map screen. If a scene wants a character to be somewhere, she can already be on her way.
