# Cindersap Forest

## What it is

The broad wooded belt south and west of town: mixed forest, a river running through it, a small lake at the southern end, and three of the valley's more solitary households scattered through the trees. It is the greenest, wettest, least tidy part of the valley and the easiest place to be alone.

## The place itself

The farm's south fence opens onto the northern edge. Follow the river down and you pass Marnie's ranch on the east side, Leah's one-room cabin close to the water, and — set apart, up a path, behind a low stone wall — the wizard's tower. Further south the ground opens into a lake with a small island and a fishing spot people are territorial about.

Two things in the forest are just ruins. A burnt-out house on the west side has nothing left but a doorframe and a story attached, and a pipe from the town's sewer surfaces in the undergrowth nearby with a hatch that needs a key. An old hollow stump on the north-west side used to be a tree; something moved into it after somebody rebuilt it with hardwood, and now there is a small industrious household in there that trades and multiplies.

At the far west edge, past fallen timber, a gap leads through to the secret woods. Near the southern lake there is also a door set into the rock that will not open for anyone who has not learned enough of the valley's five trades; behind it, apparently, is a room someone prepared for a grandchild, with tools and notes left in it.

## What it gives

Spring brings horseradish and dandelions; summer, spice berries and sweet pea; autumn, mushrooms and blackberries along the whole treeline; winter, crocus, holly and crystal fruit under snow. The river holds sunfish, catfish, bream, shad and smallmouth bass, the lake holds walleye and midnight carp in the colder half of the year, and there is a legendary winter fish in the lake that has defeated better anglers than anyone here.

Some days a covered cart parks in the clearing and sells things nobody can source locally, at prices nobody can justify.

## Life here

The spring flower dance happens in the forest clearing, and the winter ice festival on the frozen lake. For two days in high summer the riverbank fills with visiting anglers for the trout derby. Otherwise this is where the valley goes to not be watched: foraging, fishing, walking off an argument, meeting someone away from the square.

## Roleplay notes

Good for privacy, weather, and the sound of Leah's chisel carrying further than she thinks. Nothing here is on a timetable.
