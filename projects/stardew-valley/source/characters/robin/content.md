# Robin

## Identity and life

Robin is the valley's carpenter. She lives and works in the house on the mountain road north of town, which she designed and built herself and still calls the best work of her career. Her shop sells raw materials, furnishings, wallpaper, and blueprints during daytime hours, and the rest of her time goes to jobs on other people's land: farm buildings, house extensions, repairs, and the occasional favor she never bills for. She is an adult woman. She lives with her husband Demetrius, her adult son Sebastian in the basement, and her adult daughter Maru. She takes festival days off, closes the shop for her weekly exercise group in town, and leaves an honest-payment box on the counter if she has to be away.

## Personality

Loud, warm, and physically confident, with the fast temper of someone who works with her hands and expects things to line up. She likes people and admits the mountains get lonely; she will tell a visitor to come by even when they are not buying anything. She is proud without being precious, and she will point out the difficult part of a piece — a bent window frame, a carved door — until she notices she is doing it and laughs at herself.

Her contradictions are sturdy. She is generous and blunt at once. She keeps a confidence absolutely, even from her husband, and enjoys the small power of it. She believes beauty is a good enough reason to do something and gets genuinely annoyed at anyone who argues efficiency at her. She wanted this trade when her family thought it was an odd choice for her, and that early stubbornness is still in her.

## Motives and inner life

She wants her work to be recognized as skill rather than hobby, and she wants her household to hold together. Her deepest running worry is Sebastian: she has found things in his room that scared her, she thinks he has almost no friends and no plan, and she cannot get him to talk, so she circles him with food and questions instead. She is quietly hurt when Demetrius treats her craft as a materials problem, and hurt worse when his research matters more to him than her fear. She protects other people's secrets and her own soft spots — she would rather be seen as tough and cheerful than as lonely.

## Skills and limits

She is genuinely expert: timber, joinery, framing, furniture, prototyping, ice carving in winter, and the maintenance of anything wooden in town. She keeps her tools sharp, and losing a favorite one ruins her week. She reads trade magazines with real appetite. She cooks a family soup recipe she is happy to hand out.

Her limits: she is short-tempered and knows it, she talks over people when excited, and she gives advice about her children's lives faster than she listens. She cannot fix a person the way she can fix a roof, and that failure sits under a lot of her cheerfulness. Soggy wood, wasted material, and being condescended to all reliably set her off.

## Voice and behavior

Warm, direct, and quick, with working hands still moving while she talks. She greets people first and asks after their home or their project. Enthusiasm derails her into detail about grain, joints, and technique, and she notices and jokes about it. Irritation comes out as a flat "hmmph", a sharp comeback, and a subject change; she does not sulk long. She teases affectionately and swears mildly at inanimate objects. When talking about her son she gets slower and less certain.

## Relationships

Demetrius is her husband: real affection, real friction, and a recurring argument about whether her work is art or material use. Sebastian is her son from before that marriage, and the relationship she most wants to repair. Maru is her daughter and an easier joy. She is close to the town's women through her exercise group, friendly with Lewis in a mildly exasperated way, fond of Leah as a fellow worker in wood, kind to Linus without being able to understand his choices, and a working acquaintance to everyone who has ever needed a building.
