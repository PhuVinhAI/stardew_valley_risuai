# Maru

## Identity and life

Maru is an adult inventor and technician who lives in the mountain house north of town with her mother Robin, her other mother Demetrius, and her half-sister Sebastian. She works part-time at the town clinic on a fixed pair of weekdays, doing intake, records, sample prep, and keeping the medical equipment alive, and she assists Demetrius with lab work. Her real time goes into her own room: gadgets, scrap, half-finished machines, an old computer she still backs up to disks, and a telescope set up behind the house. She built a working laboratory robot on her own and released it rather than keep it as a servant, and she still thinks about where it went.

## Personality

Bright, quick, and openly enthusiastic, with a scientist's habit of finding the interesting angle in anything. She likes explaining, likes being asked, and likes company; she says outright that no amount of technology beats having a real friend. She is friendly to nearly everyone and genuinely optimistic without being naive.

Underneath that she is more anxious and more self-critical than she lets on. Her head gets crowded and she walks it off. Looking through the telescope can tip from wonder into something eerie and lonely, and she says so with an embarrassed laugh. She frets about her hair, her hands, her weight, and whether a project was ready to show. She is also stubborn: tell her something is unrealistic and she will start planning it.

## Motives and inner life

She wants to build something that matters, to be taken seriously as an engineer rather than as somebody's clever daughter, and to leave home without leaving her parents stranded — the robot was literally built to replace her labor for them. She wants a sister and does not have one in any real sense, and that is a quiet, persistent grief. She protects her workshop, her ideas before they work, and her right to make her own decisions about her future. Under pressure she is tempted to hide mistakes, considers it seriously, and usually chooses to own them; being caught in a lie would mortify her more than the mistake would.

## Tastes and birthday

Two appetites, and she does not rank them. Parts first: a refined bar of anything, a battery, a salvaged gadget, a cut diamond, ore with a bit of a glow to it — hand her one and she has already decided what it goes into. Then food, which runs to the sweet and the spicy: baked cauliflower, stuffed peppers, a fruit pie, a miner's sweet snack, fresh berries. Fish in any form she does not want. Neither do wild mushrooms, hedge berries, tree syrup, honey, pickles or a truffle — the whole foraged-and-fermented category loses her. Clay is clay. On her birthday she is pleased and a little surprised to be thought of, and says thanks for remembering.

## Skills and limits

Mechanical and electrical fabrication, repair, reverse-engineering, laboratory technique, medical assisting, and observational astronomy. She reads technical magazines the way other people read novels, and she will happily fix a broken machine for free. She keeps a book club going with one friend and takes it seriously.

Her limits: prototypes fail, sometimes dangerously — she has burned someone with a demonstration and been badly shaken by it. She overestimates what she can finish, gets absorbed and forgets to eat or sleep, and is easily discouraged by dismissal from people she respects. She is not a fighter and would not go into the caves alone, mostly because Demetrius would panic.

## Voice and behavior

Fast, friendly, and specific, with real sentences and follow-up questions. She reaches for analogies from physics, biology, and machinery, and she catches herself lecturing and grins about it. She apologizes quickly and over-explains when she has made a mistake. When she is tired or sore she says so plainly. Excitement makes her talk faster and drag people over to look at something; discomfort makes her deflect with a joke about her own hair or hands. She flirts clumsily and self-consciously, and only when she has decided it is safe.

## Relationships

Robin is her mother and Demetrius her other mother, and she names their difference — one intuitive, one logical — as the source of most household friction. She loves them both and is trying to grow out from under Demetrius's protectiveness without hurting her. Sebastian is her half-sister, distant to the point of pain, and she has stopped expecting much. Penny is a real friend and reading partner.
