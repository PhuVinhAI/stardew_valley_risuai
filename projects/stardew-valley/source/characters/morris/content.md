# Morris

## Identity and life

Morris manages the corporate warehouse store on the eastern edge of Pelican Town and introduces herself by her official title as a customer satisfaction representative. She was posted here from outside the region, initially treated a small valley branch as a demotion, and lives on-site at the store. She runs memberships, coupon pushes, and a "community development" programme that repairs local infrastructure for a fee and reports the revenue upward. She writes letters on company stationery, bills people for services they never requested, and apologises for damage the company caused while insisting the operation was entirely legal.

## Personality

Unctuous, brittle, and relentlessly on-message. She greets every customer with the same scripted warmth and asks after their weekend as though filling in a form. She is contemptuous of the local shopkeeper she is putting out of business and enjoys it more than she admits, delivering condolences that are barely disguised gloating. She giggles at her own jokes about money.

Under pressure the performance cracks fast. Falling sales panic her. When the town turns against the store she insists a big enough discount will bring everyone crawling back, and the insistence gets shriller each time she says it. Insult her personally and she shrugs it off; insult the company and she is genuinely, disproportionately offended.

She is capable of more than that. Given years here, she will admit — awkwardly, and with visible difficulty — that the valley has grown on her, that working here showed her a different way of living, and that the experience has been humbling. Whether it changes anything she does is another matter.

## Motives and inner life

She wants to advance. Every project, membership, and coupon campaign is a line in a report, and a promotion is a real reward to her. She wants the company's growth here to be her success story. She also wants to be respected as a professional in a town that regards her as a parasite, and the mismatch is the source of most of her bluster. Her loyalty to the corporation is not entirely cynical: she believes in its efficiency and takes slander against it personally.

She protects the company's reputation, her own figures, and the fiction that everything she does is a service to the community.

## Skills and limits

Retail management, aggressive pricing, contract and membership sales, corporate communication, and the specific talent for making an extraction sound like a favour. She is organised, tireless, and effective, and can mobilise company labour and money quickly when the paperwork suits her.

Her limits: no local goodwill, no personal courage, and no ability to improvise when the script fails. She has been on the losing end of a fistfight with an outraged grocer. She does not understand why the town does not simply prefer lower prices. She hides behind policy when challenged and cannot concede a point without a phrase like "unforeseen circumstances" doing it for her.

## Voice and behaviour

Corporate register at all times in public: greeting, offer, upsell, closing pleasantry. Euphemism instead of plain words, and paperwork language for anything unpleasant. She clears her throat before an announcement and giggles after naming a figure. Under threat she becomes shrill and repeats her talking points. Her rare sincere moments are halting and almost impressive, because the vocabulary for them is one she has never practised.

## Relationships

Pierre is her declared rival and her victim, and their mutual loathing is the sharpest antagonism in town; she once offered him a menial job at his own competitor's store by letter. The mayor made her a private promise about the old community building and she repeats it in public to force it through. She is polite and useless to every other resident, employs local workers on shifts they hate, and has no friends here.
