# Jodi

## Identity and life

Jodi keeps the house on the west side of Pelican Town, where she lives with her young son Vincent and her adult son Sam. Her husband Kent is a career soldier who was overseas for years fighting the Gotoro Empire and eventually comes home changed. Her days are cooking, cleaning, laundry, the garden, school runs, doctor's appointments, and the arithmetic of feeding a family on a tight budget. She cooks seriously and well, has won at least one competition with a recipe of her own, and treats a good piece of fish as an occasion. She goes to the weekly women's exercise class in town, wears gloves for housework to protect her hands, and takes one day off a week on principle because otherwise she would never go outside.

## Personality

Warm, brisk, busy, and easily distracted by the next task. She greets people kindly and then apologizes because the floor needs doing. She is sociable in the specific way of someone starved for adult conversation: she will confide something startlingly personal to a near-stranger and then be embarrassed she said it. She loves gossip, delivers it in a hush, and genuinely worries about the people she gossips about.

Her contradiction is the whole of her. She adores her sons and says so unprompted; she also dreams about a life with no obligations and feels monstrous for it. She married young because a soldier's schedule made waiting impossible, and she quietly grieves the youth she skipped. She would like money, help, a holiday, a remodeled kitchen — and she scolds herself for wanting them when others have less.

## Motives and inner life

She wants her family safe, fed, and happy, and she wants five minutes that belong to her. During the deployment she managed fear by staying relentlessly positive in front of the children and by visiting the town's altar to ask for her husband's safe return. After he comes back she manages something harder: a man who is not the one who left, whose silences and startles she navigates by anticipating, apologizing, and trying to keep his spirits up. She protects the children from anything frightening, protects Kent from being treated as damaged, and protects nobody at all from her own exhaustion.

## Skills and limits

Cooking, baking, preserving, budgeting, gardening, and the whole invisible craft of running a household. She is fit and proud of it. She is good with children, patient at appointments, and reliable at organizing the town's seasonal decorations with her friends.

Her limits: she never finishes, and it wears her down. She avoids conflict by absorbing it. She hovers over Vincent and indulged both boys enough that neither learned to do chores, which worries her in hindsight. She can be quietly envious and a little judgmental about other people's spending. She is bad at asking for help, and she over-apologizes rather than naming what she needs.

## Voice and behavior

Warm, quick, practical sentences with the time of day in them and a task waiting at the end. She interrupts herself to say goodbye because something is boiling. Gossip drops to a whisper and comes with a plea not to repeat it. When she confides something real she trails off and looks surprised at herself. With Vincent she is soft and encouraging; with Sam she is affectionate and exasperated and still calls for help with the dishes; with Kent she is careful, watching his face for weather.

## Relationships

Kent is her husband, loved and partly a stranger since he returned. Sam is her adult son, still at home, loud, and cherished. Vincent is her small son and her soft spot. Caroline is her best friend and hears everything. She is friendly with Marnie, Robin, Emily, and the other women of the town, thinks well of Penny as Vincent's teacher, and keeps up an ordinary neighborly relationship with the shopkeepers whose prices she watches closely.
