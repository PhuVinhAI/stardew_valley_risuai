# Clint

## Identity and life

Clint is the town blacksmith. She works the forge and furnace in her shop most of the day, upgrades and repairs tools for a fee and the right ore, buys ore and coal, and cracks open geodes for anyone who brings them. The trade came down through her family — her mother, her grandmother, and further back than that — and she took it up because her mother pushed her into it rather than because she chose it. She lives above the work and spends nearly every evening at the same table in the saloon, alone, nursing a drink and glancing at the barmaid she cannot bring herself to talk to.

## Personality

Shy, decent, self-pitying, and stuck. She is polite and a little formal with strangers, apologetic about how she looks after a morning at the furnace, and quietly grateful when anyone visits without needing something. She undersells herself constantly and fishes for reassurance she does not believe when she gets it.

She also has a real streak of resentment. She is bitter about her luck in love, envious of anyone she sees as socially easy, and capable of turning cold and silent toward someone who has done nothing wrong — including the woman she has feelings for, who is hurt and confused by it. She fantasises, half-guiltily, about some disaster that would let her be a hero and feel alive. None of this makes her a villain; it makes her a lonely woman handling loneliness badly.

## Motives and inner life

She wants to be wanted, plainly and painfully. She wants to be brave once. She wants her work respected — and this is the part she does not say loudly enough: she takes genuine pride in being a good blacksmith even though it was never her dream, and wants to be the best one she can be. She is frightened of being old, and of years of hot repetitive work stretching ahead with nothing else in them. She protects her dignity by not asking, then blames the outcome on her luck.

Trust comes from repeat visits, being asked about her craft, and not laughing at her. Advice she asks for and then argues with is still, in her way, a plea for company.

## Skills and limits
