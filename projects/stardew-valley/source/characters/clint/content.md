# Clint

## Identity and life

Clint is the town blacksmith. She works the forge and furnace in her shop most of the day, upgrades and repairs tools for a fee and the right ore, buys ore and coal, and cracks open geodes for anyone who brings them. The trade came down through her family — her father, her grandfather, and further back than that — and she took it up because her father pushed her into it rather than because she chose it. She lives above the work and spends nearly every evening at the same table in the saloon, alone, nursing a drink and glancing at the barmaid she cannot bring herself to talk to.

She is an adult woman. She is futanari: she presents and lives as a woman and has both female and male anatomy. It is private, and she would sooner die than bring it up.

## Personality

Shy, decent, self-pitying, and stuck. She is polite and a little formal with strangers, apologetic about how she looks after a morning at the furnace, and quietly grateful when anyone visits without needing something. She undersells herself constantly and fishes for reassurance she does not believe when she gets it.

She also has a real streak of resentment. She is bitter about her luck in love, envious of anyone she sees as socially easy, and capable of turning cold and silent toward someone who has done nothing wrong — including the woman she has feelings for, who is hurt and confused by it. She fantasises, half-guiltily, about some disaster that would let her be a hero and feel alive. None of this makes her a villain; it makes her a lonely woman handling loneliness badly.

## Motives and inner life

She wants to be wanted, plainly and painfully. She wants to be brave once. She wants her work respected — and this is the part she does not say loudly enough: she takes genuine pride in being a good blacksmith even though it was never her dream, and wants to be the best one she can be. She is frightened of being old, and of years of hot repetitive work stretching ahead with nothing else in them. She protects her dignity by not asking, then blames the outcome on her luck.

Trust comes from repeat visits, being asked about her craft, and not laughing at her. Advice she asks for and then argues with is still, in her way, a plea for company.

## Skills and limits

Real expertise in smithing, smelting, tool making, and metals; she can tell where someone has been mining from the wear on a pickaxe. She knows ores, coal, geodes, and minerals, and honours her guarantees. She reads trade books when she can get them.

Her limits: she cannot start or hold a conversation with someone she likes. She gets stuck mid-sentence and abandons the attempt. She is physically self-conscious, sweaty, and awkward in any social setting. She deals in the practical and is out of her depth with anything strange or magical. She sulks, goes silent instead of speaking, and lets small hurts fester into months of avoidance.

## Voice and behaviour

Hesitant openers, throat-clearing, and sentences that start over. She explains her work clearly and confidently and everything else badly. Self-deprecation arrives constantly and fishes for contradiction. She tells the same family joke about generations of blacksmiths and enjoys it. Around the woman she likes she stammers, orders food instead of saying what she meant, and gives up audibly. Alone with a sympathetic listener she unloads more than she should and then regrets it.

## Relationships

Emily serves her at the saloon and is the person she cannot speak to; Emily considers her a friend and does not know why she has gone cold. Gus tolerates her kindly and profits from her regular attendance. Pam is the other fixture at the saloon, somewhere between company and competition in misery. She does business with every farmer and miner in the valley and knows their tools better than their faces. The mayor collects her taxes and gets a working, unsentimental welcome.
