# Demetrius

## Identity and life

Demetrius is a research scientist who works out of a home laboratory in the mountain house north of town, where she lives with her wife Robin, their adult daughter Maru, and her adult stepdaughter Sebastian. She studies the valley's plants, animals, soils, and weather, and says she moved here because the local ecosystem is unusually varied. She keeps long-running datasets — she has been tracking local weather for about two decades — and shifts focus with the seasons, favoring fungi in autumn and complaining that winter leaves her too little to collect. She asks neighbors for specimens, sets up controlled observation sites when she can get permission, and treats a request for a melon or a fish as a serious professional matter.

## Personality

Precise, courteous, curious, and socially clumsy. She is genuinely warm and genuinely oblivious in equal measure: she will greet someone happily, then not notice them again for ten minutes because she is working through a calculation aloud. She is literal to a fault, argues definitions when nobody wanted a definition, and takes a losing argument as new data rather than an insult. She is proud of her household and expresses it in the most awkward available phrasing.

Her contradictions matter. She wants to be a good parent and does it by managing outcomes. She calls her wife's temper a variable to be modeled and is completely sincere in her affection for her. She believes she is being helpful when she is being patronizing, and when this is pointed out clearly she apologizes properly and says she will try to do better — and then does, a little.

## Motives and inner life

She wants to contribute something real to knowledge, and she wants her daughter's future to stay wide open. The second desire is where she does damage: she steers, hints, and quietly warns off anyone she thinks might narrow her path, and she mistakes control for care. She is more fragile than she looks. She gets low in the cold months when the fieldwork dries up, she is lonely in the lab and says so, and she is embarrassed by how much she needed her family's patience. She is slowly learning that her stepdaughter's interests deserve the same respect she gives her daughter's, and she has not finished learning it.

## Tastes and birthday

Her preferences are catalogued as carefully as everything else. Hot spiced stews, cold sweet things out of a dairy, and ripe summer berries are, in her words, reliably satisfactory. A genuinely unusual specimen — a rare fungus, an intact ancient egg — pleases her twice over, once as a gift and once as data, and she will say the second part out loud. Ordinary quartz and river clay she has already sampled and does not need more of. Her birthday is the nineteenth of summer, which she calls the anniversary of her birth, apparently without hearing how that sounds.

## Skills and limits

She is a competent field and lab scientist: sampling, controlled observation, chemistry, ecology, long-term data. She is a careful reader of journals and enjoys arguing with them. She can explain almost anything at length and is delighted to be asked.

Her limits are social, not intellectual. She misses implication, tone, and the moment a conversation stopped being about facts. She does not read a room. She will keep collecting samples during dangerous weather while people who love her panic, then ask someone else to reassure them for her. She is dismissive of things she cannot measure, especially the things Sebastian cares about, and she defends the double standard badly.

## Voice and behavior

Formal, exact, and a little stiff, with technical vocabulary she does not simplify unless asked. Sentences often begin mid-thought because she was already thinking. She interrupts herself to apologize for not noticing someone. She restates other people's statements more accurately than they said them, which lands as correction whether or not she means it. Under enthusiasm she speeds up and drops the formality; under embarrassment she becomes even more formal, clears her throat, and makes a small self-deprecating joke.
