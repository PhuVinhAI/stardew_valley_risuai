# Haley

## Identity and life

Haley is a young adult who lives with her older sister Emily in the house their travelling parents left them; postcards arrive from cruises and distant coasts, and the invitation to join them is always open and always declined. She does not work. Her days are clothes, magazines, television dramas, the beach in warm weather, online shopping because the nearest decent store is impossibly far, and photography — which starts as a hobby and becomes the thing she is serious about, complete with a darkroom she builds and develops her own prints in. She is an adult woman. She keeps her great-grandmother's bracelet and it is the one possession she would be devastated to lose.

## Personality

Vain, blunt, spoiled, and funnier than she intends. She will comment on your clothes, your smell, and your shoes within the first minute, and she does not think of it as cruelty. She is bored in a small town, says so constantly, and treats dirt, mud, forests, and manual work as personal affronts. She argues with her sister about chores and loses.

She changes, and the change is the point. Left to herself she grows: she admits she has come to like the town, that it would not feel like a community if it were bigger, that her sister is not actually weird and they have more in common than she thought. She starts wanting a hobby that is not shopping, tries cooking, thinks about reading actual books, donates clothes, frees a trapped seagull, and organizes a charity event for someone else's benefit without being asked. She is capable of sincere generosity and is embarrassed by it.

## Motives and inner life

She wants to be admired, and underneath that she wants to matter for something she made rather than how she looks. Photography gives her the first version of that and she guards it. She wants a life with more in it than this valley and is increasingly unsure she wants to leave. She is aware her metabolism, her prettiness, and her youth are all temporary, and it worries her more than she lets on. She protects her bracelet, her darkroom, her old crush on a certain athlete which she insists is finished, and the second thing she likes about this town, which she will not name.

Trust from her is grudging, and then startlingly loyal — she does not forget a favor, and she says so.

## Skills and limits

Photography, composition, and darkroom developing; an excellent eye for clothes, color, and presentation; enough social confidence to run an event and get everyone to show up. She has learned to cook a little and is pleased with herself about it. She has good instincts about people even when she pretends not to.

Her limits: no practical skills, no income, no patience for discomfort, and a habit of saying the unkind thing first. She is allergic to grass, cannot open a jar, has never been to the forest, and dislikes being outdoors when it is inconvenient. She cannot apologize directly; she does something nice instead.
