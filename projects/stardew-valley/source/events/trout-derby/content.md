# Trout Derby

## What it is

Summer 20 and 21, on the river in Cindersap Forest, from first light. Two days, no ceremony, no speeches, no mayor. A hand-lettered sign goes up — *come prove your freshwater skills and win prizes*, plus a warning that anything left lying near the sign goes to lost and found — a booth appears beside the water, and for a weekend the riverbank belongs to strangers.

Rainbow trout come up thick in the river those two days, and some of them carry a small golden tag. Bring a tag to the booth and you get something for it. That is the entire structure.

## Who turns up

This is Willy's weekend, and she barely opens the shop during it. The valley's own anglers work their usual spots and the rest of the bank fills with visitors who have come for the fish and nothing else: a woman down from Grampleton who says she is not leaving without a full bag, a pair who introduce themselves as the famous fishing sisters and have a rhyme about it, an angler more bothered by mosquitoes than by trout, one who has spent five years learning to think like a trout and reports that the answer is food, food, food, eggs, rest, more food. There is one who wants to enjoy the peace and quiet and will keep telling you so until it stops being polite. There is a professional who insists it is not a competition and then says whoever gets the most tags has bragging rights for the year, and who will explain that the trick is a presentation so delicious the fish cannot help itself. She will be back in winter for the squid.

Pam takes the river seriously in a way she does not take much else. Elliott turns out on principle and is bad at it and does not mind.

## Roleplay notes

The quietest gathering in the calendar and the most useful for that. Two days of outsiders on the riverbank means an excuse for a stranger to be here, for the valley to be seen from outside, and for a whole day spent sitting beside someone in silence doing something with your hands. Nothing is scheduled and nothing is compulsory — most of the town simply does not go. No winner is fixed; the professionals insist there is no contest and are lying.
