# Egg Festival

## What it is

Spring 13, the town square, morning until mid-afternoon. The first festival of the year and the one everybody treats as the season opening: pastel bunting over the cobbles, a stall run out of the square, and a decorated egg on every flat surface. Gus has been cooking for days and everything on the table is eggs — fried, boiled, poached, deviled, scrambled, chocolate — plus a fruit punch that Pam has done something to and asked you not to mention.

## The egg hunt

The centrepiece. The mayor makes a speech about how efficiently the town's participants collect eggs compared with how they collect litter, tells everyone to calm down, asks whether everyone is ready, and starts it. Then the square is chaos for a couple of minutes: coloured eggs hidden in the flower beds, behind the benches, under the notice board, and grown women moving faster than they would like witnessed. Abigail has loved it for years, wins it more often than not, and has begun to wonder aloud whether she is too old for it — she is not going to stop. The winner gets a prize handed over with ceremony and the mayor thanks everyone for coming.

Nobody plays entirely fair. Willy hides an egg so well some years that nobody finds it until the summer heat brings out the smell. There used to be a rotten egg toss; the mayor put an end to that with some speed. Jas and Vincent have won it once between them by pooling what they found.

## Who is where

Emily is up before dawn colouring eggs and considers that the best part of the season; some years she sews tiny hats and trousers for a few of them. Marnie takes the whole thing as a credit to her hens and says so. Shane's birds have been laying overtime and she thinks they have earned an evening of sweet corn. Caroline watches Abigail and remembers her at four. Evelyn calls the tradition older than she is and means the comfort of it. Linus turns up quietly, late, for the food, and says plainly that nobody talks to her; she is after the deviled eggs and the pie. Sam's allergies ruin it for her every single year. Pierre is behind her booth and will bark at anyone who wanders round the back of it.

There is a photograph stand with a bunny theme that Haley uses. Demetrius finds the eating of eggs conceptually strange and one year contributes a carp roe pie on a technicality. Alex eats a dozen too many and regrets it. Sebastian remembers the egg toss fondly. George complains, then admits the smoked food is not bad.

## Roleplay notes

Bright, silly, and warm — the least loaded festival of the year, which makes it a good place to put two people who need an easy first afternoon. The hunt gives anyone permission to be undignified in public. The punch gives them permission to be honest. Who wins is not fixed, and neither is whether the hunt happens at all: the square works perfectly well as an ordinary spring party.
