# Festival of Ice

## What it is

Winter 8, out on the frozen lake in the forest near Marnie's land, morning until mid-afternoon. A celebration of winter, which in practice means everyone standing about in the cold building things out of snow while a fishing contest happens on the ice. There is no food at all. Gus apologises for that and tells everyone to wait for the Winter Star, when they will be stuffed for days.

Robin carves the ice — parapets and sculpture — and asks not to be interrupted, because it needs her whole attention; she calls ice a rewarding challenge and a material she is not used to. Leah works alongside her and talks about impermanence being what makes the art better, and about tempering ice before you cut it or you cannot shape it at all. A travelling merchant sets up a stall with winter furniture, a rarecrow, hot food, and a Winter Star tree for anyone thinking ahead.

Everyone else makes snowmen. Emily gives hers a top hat and a scarf and insists it has to look the part. Abigail makes a snow-goon and wants your opinion of it. Maru says she is not creative about snowmen and then explains the engineering of a level foundation. Penny will ask what kind you should build together and go along with whatever you say, unless you tell her to think outside the box, in which case she will politely stay conventional. Sam and Vincent build one together and Sam gets defensive if you laugh before it is finished. Clint cannot get the snow to form balls and concludes she is better in heat. Evelyn makes one with Alex and says out loud that this could be the last time, and that life is short.

## The ice fishing contest

Holes are cut in the ice and the lake is stocked. Two minutes, provided rods, and whoever lands the most fish wins. The mayor explains the rules twice, says *begin*, and the whistle goes.

Willy nearly always wins, and everyone knows it. Pam enters every year, takes it more seriously than she takes most things, blames her spot when she loses, and says Willy is impossible to beat because she does this day and night. Elliott enters to be sporting, is frankly bad at it, and does not mind; she brings a flask of spirits to sip just before the whistle and calls it her secret weapon. Sebastian has money on the outcome with Marlon. Marlon rates Willy a tough competitor and asks whether you think you can beat her.

When it is over the mayor gags at the smell of the pile, hands over the prize, thanks everyone for coming, and tells them to put the poor fish back.

## Who else is where

Evelyn tells the story of how she met George at this festival — George threw a snowball at her, then came over to check she was all right, which Evelyn has counted as good manners for sixty years and treats as the origin of the marriage. George grumbles about being dragged into the cold and about needing a warm hat now she is old and bald. Linus builds an igloo and explains that she learned it from tundra dwellers beyond the frozen sea many years ago, and that an igloo makes a fine home but her tent is easier. Sebastian says she never gets cold and means it. Jodi says the cold brings people together. Kent likes winter because it is peaceful. Marnie feels sorry for all the cold animals out there and, some years, thinks the bundled-up piglet is adorable. Haley brings her camera for the light. Abigail lets slip that she is wearing the Winter Star sweater under her coat and asks you not to tell Pierre, who has bought the whole family matching ones.

## Roleplay notes

Cold, bright, and companionable — the festival with the least at stake and the most standing around, which makes it the easiest place for a long conversation. Two women building one snowman is a scene. Losing the contest is normal; winning it against Willy is a real event. The lake is frozen and Penny will not walk on it, which is worth remembering before anyone suggests it.
