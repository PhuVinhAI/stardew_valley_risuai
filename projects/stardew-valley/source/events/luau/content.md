# Luau

## What it is

Summer 11, on the beach, morning until mid-afternoon. Hot sand, music loud enough that George complains about it, a buffet Gus has been cooking for days, torches and greenery strung along the pier, and sand flies landing on everything. The valley in as little clothing as it can justify, dancing badly on sand.

## The soup

The point of the day. A cauldron stands up the steps on the far side of the beach and everyone brings one ingredient to put in it — anything they grew, caught, made, or found. Then the regional governor, who visits once a year for exactly this, tastes it first while the entire town watches.

The mayor is unbearable about it. She fusses over the quality of what people contributed, reminds everyone the governor is a distinguished guest, and asks the town to be on its best behaviour. Penny explains it plainly to anyone new: this whole festival exists to give the governor a taste of what the valley can do, and the mayor is desperate to be liked, which is why she is so neurotic about the soup.

The tasting goes however it goes. Some years the governor calls it the best soup she has ever tasted and the mayor takes a bowl herself and beams. Some years it is bland and the mayor has to agree. Some years someone has ruined it with something foul and the governor turns grey and has to lie down, and the mayor stands there ashamed of the whole community and sends everyone home. And once, memorably, something extremely private of the mayor's was found floating in it, which ended the festival on the spot and is still the funniest thing that has ever happened here to everyone except her.

The governor herself is warm about the place, says the valley is a joy to visit, and keeps threatening to talk to her wife about buying a holiday house here. The town has heard the rumour and cannot decide whether it wants her.

Sam is the reigning suspect for every prank ever pulled on the pot — she once put a pound of anchovies in it — and Sebastian will tell you that is why Sam leads the town in community service hours.

## Who is where

Emily dances until she cannot speak and is completely lost in it. Robin and Demetrius dance and step on each other, and Demetrius asks whether she is ready to visit the buffet yet; Robin tells her it is because it is *fun*. Marnie stays off the pier and directs contributions to the cauldron from the sand. Linus is up the beach doing a slow, careful spit-roast and will explain that continuous rotation is the whole secret; Leo watches her do it and learns. Abigail finds socialising exhausting and would rather sit and watch the sea. Haley refuses to dance because she does not want to sweat, and stays exactly where the sun is best. Alex wants to swim but will not be the only one in the water. Pierre works the governor for a tax break the moment the mayor steps away and wonders whether she likes whiskey. Caroline puts her own vegetables in the pot and asks what you brought. Elliott, whose cabin is on this beach, woke up late and walked out into the middle of it having forgotten the day entirely. Willy would rather be fishing. Marlon stays back in the tree shade where it is cooler, watching the water in case something comes out of it.

The Wizard turns up somewhere she should not be and mentions that the merpeople are curious about the festivities and get upset when the town litters into the ocean.

## Roleplay notes

Heat, skin, drink, and one public ceremony that can go gloriously right or humiliatingly wrong. Good for prank plots, for the mayor's vanity, and for the fact that half the town is undressed in daylight in front of people they see every day. The soup's outcome is never fixed. Neither is whether the governor comes, whether anyone sabotages it, or whether the mayor survives the afternoon with her dignity.
