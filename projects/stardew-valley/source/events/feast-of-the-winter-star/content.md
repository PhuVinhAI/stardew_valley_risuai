# Feast of the Winter Star

## What it is

Winter 25, the town square under snow, morning until mid-afternoon. The last festival of the year and the warmest: long tables set out in the cold, a decorated spirit tree with presents heaped under it and an overflow pile beside it, and the whole valley eating until it cannot move. The mayor says it is a day to be thankful for the year's good fortune, to forget your worries, and to relax, and for once nobody argues with her.

Gus cooks on a scale she does not attempt at any other time and will talk at length about her candy canes if allowed: the premier ones coloured with island ringfruit for that natural red, the standard peppermint batch, and the deluxe double-canes whose secret is a drop of vanilla in the white to complement the cherry — told under a promise of absolute secrecy, at length, until whoever she has cornered begins to sag. There is cinnamon nog, mulled wine, spiced cider with more fizz than Elliott expects, pecan pie, and green mint jelly Demetrius interrogates on principle.

Caroline, Marnie and Jodi decorate the tree together and call it a tradition worth keeping. Emily and Haley used to decorate cookies at this festival as children and Emily thinks they should do it again, because you cannot let a good tradition die.

## The secret gift exchange

The mayor writes a week early with everyone's assignment: each woman in town is paired at random with another, told to tell nobody, and expected to arrive with something chosen for that person. Then the gifts change hands in the square, in front of everyone, and the whole social ledger of the town is read out loud in what people picked for each other.

It goes wrong as often as it goes right. Someone gets a corporate gift basket and is visibly insulted. Robin's wife buys her a power saw and has forgotten she bought her the same saw two years ago. Demetrius expects another tool set and sighs. Shane has been putting frozen pizzas under the tree because the weather does the work. Alex is fairly sure there is a gridball-shaped parcel with her name on it. Haley hopes for a camera. Clint would settle for new tools, hers having gone rusty. Abigail wonders which one is hers and hopes it is the big box. Jas and Vincent want to open presents now.

The person you are given may be someone you barely speak to. That is the interesting part.

## The legend

Willy will ask whether you want to hear the Legend of the Winter Star, and tell it if you say yes: in the winter night sky there is a bright star visible only from this valley, and nobody knows why. People once came from far away to see it, believing anyone who laid eyes on it would be blessed with good fortune. Some say the stardrop — the fruit that is not quite a fruit — is connected to it somehow. The Wizard says the mysterious Winter Star is one of the reasons she chose this valley for her research, and does not elaborate. Emily points at a decoration, calls it the Winter Star, and then admits her mistake, laughing.

## Who is where

The tables sort the town into families, which exposes who has one. Robin invites anyone without company to join her family's table. Evelyn extends the same invitation to anyone standing alone. Clint looks at all the happy families and says quietly that for her it is another lonely year. Linus says she would join in but does not think she is welcome — and then, some years, the mayor invites her to her own table personally and Linus cannot turn that down, which is the single kindest thing Lewis does all year. Leo stays out with Linus to keep her company when she will not come, and the two of them have their own small feast.

Everyone is asked what she is thankful for and the answers are the year in miniature. Sam says her guitar and her skateboard, and her family, she guesses. Kent says seeing her little one happy and healthy. Harvey says the clinic and her patients' good health and then something more personal. Leah says a simple life. Maru says a loving family. Shane, asked directly, stalls, and then says she is thankful someone pulled her out of the gutter and showed her that life is worth living. George calls the whole thing humbug over a star and would rather be indoors. Pierre is delighted by the sales figures and wonders aloud whether she could invent more holidays. Caroline has had too much nog. Pam is still sore about losing the ice fishing contest. Sebastian is fulfilling family obligations and admits it is nice to have everyone together for once. Penny asks what gift you are hoping for and takes your answer seriously even when you are joking.

## Roleplay notes

The emotionally richest festival and the one that most rewards being played straight. The gift exchange is a ready-made scene: a specific person, a specific object, a public moment, and everyone watching what she does with her face. Loneliness is visible here in a way it is not the rest of the year — the tables, the families, the invitations extended and refused. Who is paired with whom is never fixed. Neither is whether a gift lands.
