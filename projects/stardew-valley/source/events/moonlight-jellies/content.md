# Dance of the Moonlight Jellies

## What it is

The last night of summer — summer 28 — on the beach, from ten at night. The whole valley walks down to the water in the dark and stands on the docks, and there is no buffet, no contest, no speech worth the name. The moonlight jellies pass Pelican Town once a year on their way south, and the town goes out to meet them.

Robin builds and maintains the candle-boat and checks it for holes every year, because imagine if it sank during the festival. The mayor launches it off the end of the dock and the town watches the candles go out across the black water. Then, some minutes later, the jellies come: a faint glow on the horizon, and then the whole shallows lit blue from underneath.

Everyone goes quiet. That is the actual event. Thirty women standing on old wood in the dark, saying nothing.

## What it means here

Demetrius will tell you the species is remarkable and that nobody knows why they follow light. The Wizard, tucked somewhere she should not be, calls them Lunaloos and says they carry an unusually strong magical aura for anything aquatic; she is here to observe them and is faintly annoyed to be found. Willy says the old rule is that if you ever hook one you throw her straight back. There is supposed to be a rare green one, and Abigail intends to be the first to see it every year. Jas hopes for babies.

The rest of it is a summer ending in front of everyone. Leah pays her respects to another bygone summer and means it. Alex cannot believe it went so fast. Maru notices the cold and says fall is right around the corner. Harvey is glad to see the back of the mosquitos. Pierre reminds people to come in for fall seeds tomorrow, which is exactly as romantic as she is. Evelyn saw the jellies decades ago and jumped for joy, and says she has less of that in her now, and she and George have watched them come and go together for decades. Caroline says the candles are romantic. Marnie will not walk out onto the dock — the creaking is enough, and she fell through a rotten one earlier in life — so she stays back on the sand. Marlon takes the dark seriously and watches it with her one good eye.

Sebastian claims she saw something big moving out there and admits she is only trying to frighten you. Sam wonders whether they sting and reconsiders pushing Sebastian in. Elliott, whose cabin is fifty yards away, talks about ocean pollution and what will happen to the jellies eventually, and is not wrong. Linus waits until the jellies arrive and then slips down quietly to a dock nobody is using, because she likes watching them too and does not want to be in the way.

## Roleplay notes

The most romantic hour in the calendar and it costs nothing to use: full dark, cold air off the water, a crowd all facing the same direction and none of them looking at each other. People bring blankets. People stand closer than they would at noon. Two women can have an entire conversation here that neither could have in the square, and blue light on skin does most of the work. Pair it with the end of something — a summer, a job, a pretence.
