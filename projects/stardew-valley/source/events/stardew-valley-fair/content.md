# Stardew Valley Fair

## What it is

Fall 16, the town square, morning until mid-afternoon, and the largest thing that happens here all year. The mayor writes a week in advance rather than a day, because there is work to do. Tents go up over the whole square, tourists come in by bus from across the country, and Pelican Town spends one day being looked at by strangers and enjoying it enormously.

There is barbecue all day and it is free, compliments of the mayor — Gus works four grills at once, puts her sauce in everything, and refuses to say what is in it no matter who asks. Animals are brought in and penned: chickens, cows and calves, pigs, rabbits, sheep, goats. Everything is louder than the town normally is.

## The grange displays

The competition that matters. Each business or household sets out its best nine things in a stand and is judged on quality and variety, and the mayor leaves an empty stand for anyone new. Pierre has been setting aside her finest produce for weeks and is completely serious about winning. Caroline supplies part of it out of her own glasshouse and has been growing for this all year. Marnie knows her display is weaker than Pierre's and hopes the judges give her something for the enormous cheese wheel. Clint lays out her best pieces, tells you she takes real pride in her work, and asks you to look closely. Willy brings iridium-quality fish. Robin's woodwork is there and she calls the craft an ancient tradition she is proud to be part of; Leah admires it openly and uses the same techniques in her own work, and the two of them end up teaching each other.

Judging is public. First place gets a rating read out, a ribbon, and a thousand star tokens; the lower places get less and a suggestion to try again next year. The mayor reminds every single winner to go and clean out her box.

## The games

Star tokens are the fair's currency, sold at fifty a piece and won at the tents. A slingshot range. A wheel of chance where you wager tokens and lose them — Sam bets and loses everything and says she should have gone green. A strength machine that rates you on a scale running from *plankton* and *wet tissue* up through *George's knee* and *soggy spaghetti* to *bodybuilder*, *mountain troll* and *mammoth*; Alex plays it until her arms give out and eats a sandwich for every hundred rounds. A fishing tent Elliott comes out of smelling of fish and delighted with a personal best. Penny won the wheel ten times running one year and is no longer allowed to play.

The token stall trades for prizes: a rarecrow, a hat, a rug, a dried sunflower arrangement, seeds, and — for anyone who has never bought one before — a stardrop.

## The fortune teller

Welwick sets her stand up in the graveyard, of all places, and reads fortunes for a hundred gold. She is a genuine diviner with a television show and the Wizard's professional respect — the two have known each other a long time and the Wizard calls her a good friend and a remarkable one. What she gives people is uncomfortably specific: a crystal ball view of who you will end up beside, whether you are playing games with people who trust you, whether you end up alone in a plush chair surrounded by luxury and desperation. She tells Haley something *interesting* and Haley giggles about it all day. Sebastian comes away from her stand not knowing what to make of it. She reads one couple's future as a happy life together. She always finishes by saying the future is not set in stone and can still be changed if you set your heart on it, and once she has read you she will not read you again — she says so rudely.

## Who else is where

There is a clown, hired on a budget, and she is not funny; Jodi assumes the mayor cut costs, Kent waits for her to do something and is disappointed, Emily thinks she would have made a better one and may have missed her calling, and the Wizard studies her and says she knows her from somewhere — the school of illusion, perhaps, or the Carnival of Secrets.

Tourists wander about being pleasant and eating too much: the biggest cheese wheel they have ever seen, a long bus ride but worth it, a fine-looking town with a real sense of community. Some of them litter and Jodi takes it personally. Sandy shuts the Oasis and travels in for the day, which is one of the few times Emily gets to see her. Shane has the animals fed and content and tells anyone to go ahead and pet them. Linus watches the animals and observes that they never judge anyone by their looks, which is more than she can say for people. Marlon has come to look at Clint's blades, which get sharper every year, along with her prices. Evelyn wishes there were a vegetarian option and suggests zucchini skewers. Harvey stands two steps from a rack of pork ribs having a crisis of professional conscience.

## Roleplay notes

Crowded, loud, competitive, and full of strangers — the best day of the year for a chance encounter, a rivalry, a public embarrassment, or a fortune that lands too close to the bone. The grange result is never fixed and neither is who wins what. Welwick is a good device precisely because she says the thing a character is refusing to say, and then leaves.
