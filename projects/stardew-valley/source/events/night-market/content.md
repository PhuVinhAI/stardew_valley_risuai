# Night Market

## What it is

Winter 15, 16 and 17, on the beach and the pier, from five in the evening until late. A fleet of travelling merchants puts in at Pelican Town for three nights and turns the dark beach into a lit market. It is the newest tradition here and the town took to it fast. The notice promises free coffee, which is most of why some people come.

Boats moor along the pier with lanterns strung between them and each one sells something different. One deals in outdoor decoration and asks whether anything catches your eye. One is unsettling — a blue-lit boat whose keeper speaks in a whisper and stocks something new every night, rare seeds, strange furniture, a piano — and Emily says it is her favourite because it is so unique. Even the travelling cart merchant who works the forest road comes by sea for this, and reports that her pig likes the swim. The desert trader shuts up shop and comes in for it.

## What there is to do

**The submarine.** A captain runs a deep-sea tour out of the end of the pier and takes people down for an hour of fishing in water nobody sees otherwise. Willy says the captain is an old friend. Demetrius highly recommends it and will tell you about the creatures at that depth. Maru is more interested in the machine than the fish. Sam decides that if everything else fails she will become a submarine captain, which might be cool.

**The mermaid show.** In a small house at the end of the beach, five performers put on a show by lamplight to a strange, slow song. Something is hidden in it — there is a crumpled note that circulates in the valley giving five numbers in a particular order and nothing else by way of explanation, and anyone who works out what to do with them gets something out of the sea for it. Alex says the show is her favourite thing about the whole market. Clint wants to see it to calm her nerves before a doctor's appointment and immediately panics about what people will think she is looking at. Jas is not old enough and says so sadly.

**The painter.** A woman who introduces herself as the famous painter Lupini sells exactly one canvas a night for twelve hundred gold and will not sell you a second. Leah studies one and allows that a Lupini original is not bad.

**A shrouded figure** at the edge of the light will send you home for two hundred and fifty gold and does not explain how.

## Who is where

Caroline does all her winter shopping here and asks you to warn her if you see Pierre coming. Marnie finds fine purple silk and points out you cannot get that at the warehouse store. Lewis says she has bought some of her favourite things at this market. Robin loves the boats themselves and the creaking of wet wood. Sebastian is more relaxed at night and might even buy a trinket. Penny stands in the spiced air and asks if you can smell it. Harvey says winter is a dreary season and this makes it brighter. Abigail finds the whole thing mysterious and loves it. Evelyn calls it one of the newer traditions and asks George whether they have not taken to it rather quickly; George is there for the free coffee. Elliott is enchanted by the idea of a merchant fleet travelling the world for riches. Jodi sighs about how thrilling it must be. Linus feels a kinship with them as fellow travellers and is content to stand and watch. Leo just likes looking at the lights, and says it reminds her of something long ago. Shane takes the night off the saloon for Jas's sake.

Willy cannot sleep with the market going on right outside her shop, and shrugs it off — she is flexible — and on the third night says there is nothing like a hot drink after a cold, dark day.

## Roleplay notes

Three consecutive evenings of lantern light, cold sand, unfamiliar merchants, and a town out after dark with money to spend. The best-lit romantic setting in the calendar after the jellies and far less solemn. Good for buying someone something, for the submarine as an hour alone underwater, for the mermaid show as a shared secret, and for the fact that nobody is where they usually are. Nothing has to be bought and nothing has to be found.
