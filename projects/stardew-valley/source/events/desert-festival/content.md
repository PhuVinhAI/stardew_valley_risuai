# Desert Festival

## What it is

Spring 15, 16 and 17, out in Calico Desert, from mid-morning each day. Three days, and the only festival that takes the valley out of the valley — which means it happens only if the bus is running. It has been a tradition for as long as anyone can remember and it stopped for years when the bus broke down. Getting it back is the single most popular thing anyone has ever done here, and the mayor thanks whoever managed it every time she sees them.

Everything is free. The whole town comes out into permanent summer with the sand hot through their shoes, and stays for three days.

## What happens out there

**The eggs.** Calico eggs — palm-sized, patterned, worthless as food — turn up all over the sand and in the caverns, and for three days they are the local currency. Nobody accepts money for anything; they accept eggs. This is the whole economy of the festival and people take it seriously enough to bargain hard over a handful.

**The stalls.** Two townspeople are picked each day to run a stall out of whatever they happen to own, which produces the best social document of the year: Abigail selling things out of her own closet, Sam offering a broken skateboard with a straight face, Leah's mini bookcases, Harvey's model aeroplanes, Maru's spare iridium, Marnie's leftover roll of wallpaper, Jodi apologising for her selection, Evelyn happy to sit there all day, Pam clearing old bottles out of the back of the fridge, Shane's freezer contents with a warning about the dates, George claiming to have everything worth selling. Pierre sells eggs for cash and asks you not to tell the mayor.

**The races.** Five racers — a rooster, a hornet, a shoe-shaped something, a snail, a cactus crawler — an announcer who works the crowd, and open betting on the winner. There is also a woman at the edge of the crowd who will fix a race for one egg and makes a phone call afterwards. The snail winning is a running joke and the announcer has never recovered from it.

**The food.** Gus is off duty for the first time all year and delighted about it. A chef with an unsettling manner will build you something to order from a short list of ingredients and sauces and then name the result — *Nachos Of The Desert*, *Warrior Smoothie*, *Smell Of The Sea* — and hand it over. There is a spicy tent that George makes straight for. Twelve soda flavours, which Shane intends to get through all of.

**The rest.** A scholar with a quiz and a prize for anyone who answers four questions about the valley. A woman giving away small cacti to anyone she decides is ready for the responsibility. A makeover stand where Emily or Sandy will rebuild your whole look and hand your old clothes back in a bag. The Adventurer's Guild sets up a challenge booth — Marlon offering a pair of dares each day, Gil awake for once and grading whatever you drag out of the Skull Cavern. Willy runs desert fishing challenges from the dry riverbed and pays in eggs.

## Who is where

Sandy calls it the busiest weekend of her year and means it as joy — she sees everyone she knows in three days and then does not see them again. Emily is at the makeover stand and gets to spend actual time with her. Robin stands around talking to neighbours she never sees. Haley oils herself and commits fully to the desert. Elliott has become a connoisseur of sand and will explain. Linus rides out under the bus. Kent is out in the open, in heat, being asked whether she is enjoying it. Harvey stays all day in case somebody does something stupid in the caverns and cheerfully tells you she would advise against it.

## Roleplay notes

Three days, so it is the one festival with room for a whole story rather than an afternoon. The heat, the distance from home, and the free food loosen everyone — this is where people say things they would not say in the square. Whether the bus runs is a live question and a good hook. The egg economy is a texture, not a system: nobody needs to count.
