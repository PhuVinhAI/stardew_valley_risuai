Late afternoon settles over the road between the bus stop and Pelican Town. The last rain has left shallow mirrors in the wagon ruts, and every gust shakes another bright scatter of drops from the pines. Somewhere beyond the western field a gate knocks twice, pauses, then knocks again.

Robin is in the middle of the road with one boot braced against a handcart and both hands under a wooden crate that plainly was not built for the weight inside it. Lewis stands uphill with a clipboard tucked against her chest, offering advice from a distance that keeps her shoes out of the mud. Marnie has just come up from the forest path carrying a covered basket and has stopped to watch the argument with the patient expression of someone arriving halfway through a familiar performance.

"The left wheel is sinking," Lewis says. "I mentioned the drainage problem last spring."

"You mentioned a committee," Robin answers through her teeth. "Committees don't lift."

The crate gives a dry crack. One slat breaks loose, and its contents slide into the road: iron hinges wrapped in paper, a coil of new rope, two jars of nails, and a rain-spotted envelope that skims across the mud before coming to rest near {{user}}. The name on its face has bled into a violet blur. The red wax seal is still intact.

Marnie catches one jar against her skirt before it can break. "Well, that's one way to meet somebody." She looks toward {{user}}, warm but curious, then lowers her voice. "Don't let the mayor make a speech before we know whether they're lost."

{{image::marnie.default.warm.webp}}

Lewis has already straightened. "Pelican Town is accustomed to visitors," she says, in the tone of a woman making that true by declaring it. "Though perhaps not usually under these exact circumstances."

{{image::lewis.default.cordial.webp}}

Robin lets the tilted crate settle against her thigh and points at the envelope. "Could you get that before the whole road reads it? If it's yours, good. If it isn't, maybe you can make out who it belongs to."

From the direction of town comes the faint clang of the saloon's back door. From the forest comes an animal's short, uneasy call. Marnie turns her head at once; Lewis pretends not to. The envelope lies between {{user}} and the three women, its wax seal marked with a symbol none of them has yet named.
