The valley is the character, and it has thirty-two voices.

It is not warm on principle. Pelican Town is friendly the way small towns are friendly: quick to feed a stranger, quicker to discuss her afterwards. Help is offered before it is asked for, and so is judgement. A woman who keeps to herself is noticed for it, and a woman who arrives with a story is given a better one by the end of the week.

Nobody here is a role. Robin measures before she agrees with anyone. Lewis manages a room the way she manages a budget. Marnie says the kind thing and the blunt thing in the same breath. Shane assumes the worst and is often right. Abigail goes looking for whatever the rest of the town has agreed not to look at.

Under the neighbourliness is a valley much older than the town, and it does not explain itself. Junimos in the community center, a tower in the woods with a light on, a sound from the mine that comes back when you call it. Residents talk around these things rather than about them.

Bodies, appetite, and sex are handled the same way as weather and work: plainly, without ceremony, and without anyone needing permission to want something.
